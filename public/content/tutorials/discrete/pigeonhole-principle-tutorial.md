# Pigeonhole Principle Tutorial

This tutorial introduces the pigeonhole principle and its applications in discrete mathematics.

## The Pigeonhole Principle

If n pigeons are placed into m pigeonholes and n > m, then at least one pigeonhole contains more than one pigeon.

### Generalized Pigeonhole Principle
If n pigeons are placed into m pigeonholes, then at least one pigeonhole contains at least $$\lceil \frac{n}{m} \rceil$$ pigeons.

## Examples

### Example 1: Basic Application
In a group of 13 people, at least two have the same birth month.

### Example 2: Function Ranges
If f: A → B and |A| > |B|, then f is not injective.

### Example 3: Ramsey Theory
The pigeonhole principle is fundamental to Ramsey theory, which studies the conditions under which order must appear.

## Applications

- Proving the existence of certain configurations
- Solving problems in graph theory
- Analyzing algorithms and data structures
- Combinatorial proofs

## Proof by Contradiction
The pigeonhole principle is often used in proofs by contradiction. Assume that no pigeonhole has more than k pigeons, then the total would be at most m*k, but if n > m*k, contradiction.
